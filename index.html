Hey hey!

My name is Asia (Ah-sya), and I’m 17 from Indianapolis, Indiana! I love understanding how systems are built, from the technical components to the underlying design decisions, and asking who it is those decisions actually serve. That’s most of why I love computer science, policy, entrepreneurship, and thinking about how they intersect.

I’m interested not just in whether a system works, but in how it works, what it assumes, and who those assumptions leave behind. Growing up, my dad ran a small medical transportation business, and as a kid, I often rode along while he drove patients to and from appointments. I got to know many of them through their stories and began noticing how differently they experienced the same healthcare system.

Above how convoluted these systems seemed, what struck me more was the countless assumptions they made about the people navigating them. It assumed they had time to spare, knew the right questions to ask, understood the language being used, or had someone to help when they didn’t. It often felt as though there was an assumed “typical” person on the other side of every interaction. The people I met rarely fit that picture, but they were still expected to navigate the system as if they did.

Close to my heart for this very reason, I’m now part of an extraordinary engineering team @ Brevy, where we’re building technology to help families navigate Medicaid without having to become experts in the system themselves, all while designing around the reality that the people using these systems don’t all start from the same place. Working on this has only made me more curious about the question behind it all:

how can we build systems that meet people where they are and truly design for the diversity of the human experience?

While I don’t yet have a clean answer, I’ve followed it through my work in AI-education policy, youth gun violence advocacy, building the eldercare guide @ Brevy, projects like StateScope, and more, and I’d like to keep chasing it. If you’re following it too, I’d love to talk.
