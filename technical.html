what i've built & researched

I love working to understand how things work! I’ve been doing research since I was 14, and what keeps me going is the chance to learn something new and turn it into something useful. This page brings together my research, my work on the engineering team at Brevy, and my technical projects, each one a result of following my curiosity as far as it would take me.

  • 01 Research + What I love most about research is working on questions no one has answered yet. There’s no guide to follow, so you have to figure out what to ask, how to test it, and what the results actually mean. I’ve done research in microbiology, neutrino physics, and deep learning, and while the subjects have changed, that feeling hasn’t.

    Field mapping, and my first work on Project-8

    I came to the WYSE Young Scholars program after my first lab knowing I wanted to build the tools behind the research. At the Liu Lab, I got to do exactly that, writing the code that mapped magnetic fields in 3D and controlled a magnetic levitation setup. It was also where I was introduced to Project-8, an international effort to measure the mass of the neutrino. Contributing to something that large, that quickly, was a feeling I loved.

    FieldMagnetic field mapping and levitation
    Built3D field mapping and rotational velocity analysis in MATLAB; automated data acquisition and control in Python

    Research software for a live experiment

    After my summer at UIUC, I was eager to keep working on Project-8, so I cold emailed researchers across the consortium. Dr. Hamish Robertson at the University of Washington and Dr. Penny Slocum at Yale both wrote back, and that started two of the most formative years of my life research-wise. I began with analysis work and ended up building the infrastructure behind it: containerized pipelines that let collaborators at 17 research sites run identical, reproducible analyses. I also optimized the magnet arrangement for the experiment’s electron trap, cutting magnetic field nonuniformity by about 87%. Presenting that work to a 144-member international collaboration taught me to explain complex ideas clearly and to hold my work to the standard of a live experiment.

    Non-invasive material identification

    Identifying what a complex material is made of usually means using mass spectrometry, which is expensive and destroys the sample. I’m working on an alternative: deep learning models, grounded in quantum mechanics, that identify materials from their fluorescence instead. The specifics are under NDA, but the goal is faster, cheaper analysis that leaves the sample intact.

    This work brings together everything I’ve built toward so far. It draws on the physics I learned through Project-8, the research software experience I’ve gained over years of building tools for experiments, and my growing interest in machine learning. I’ve taken on more responsibility here than in any lab before, and it’s the work I’m most excited about.

  • 02 Brevy + AI systems that help families navigate caregiving and healthcare coverage in all 50 states, reaching 1.6M+ weekly search impressions and 1,050+ weekly helpline users.

    The Brevy Eldercare Guide and Helpline

    Software engineer, multi-agent systems · May 2026 to now
    the gap
    Families in the middle of caregiving decisions have to figure out Medicaid, Medicare, VA benefits, and the cost of care, all under rules that differ in every state, often while caring for someone at the same time.
    what i did
    I engineer the production multi-agent system behind the Brevy Eldercare Guide, and I co-designed the Brevy Eldercare Helpline.
    how it works
    • Agents that handle each guide from start to finish: researching, writing, reviewing, optimizing, publishing, and keeping it current
    • A helpline built on the same research, using retrieval over the guides so answers stay grounded in what’s been reviewed
    • Questions the guides don’t yet answer become new guides after review, so the helpline and guides keep improving each other
    where it landed
    Weekly Google search impressions grew 38 times in under two months, from 42,000 to more than 1.6 million, and are still climbing. Within two weeks of launching in early September 2026, the helpline reached more than 1,050 weekly users.
  • 03 Projects + Independent projects that started with a problem I wanted to solve, ranging from national policy tracking to multi-agent experiments

    StateScope

    An explorer for AI-in-education policy · React, Vite, Python, Flask, Claude API
    the gap
    I started StateScope after my work on Indiana House Bill 1296, when I realized how hard it was to see what other states were doing. AI-in-education policy was scattered across 50 states and DC, split between laws, executive orders, and guidance, with no single place to compare them.
    what i built
    • The map. More than 75 AI-in-education bills, executive orders, and pieces of state guidance across all 50 states and DC, in one place.
    • An assistant that cites its sources. A Claude-powered Q&A that finds the relevant policies, answers with citations, and makes clear whether something is law or only guidance.
    • Data that stays current. Collection runs through a Claude research pipeline and the Congress.gov API, with every record checked against 12 topic categories.
    where it landed
    A central reference for policymakers, educators, and advocates trying to keep up with a fast-moving area of policy. Live, code.

    ASPER

    AI Sensitivity Parametric Experiment Runner · Python, OpenAI API
    the gap
    Parametric sweep experiments mean repeating the same setup, code, and analysis for every variation, which takes time away from the actual question being tested.
    what i built
    A multi-agent system that runs these experiments from start to finish. You describe the experiment in plain language, and it turns that into a structured plan, researches the problem, writes and runs the code, creates the visualizations, and produces a report.
    see it
    Live, code.

    Sudoku solver

    Solves a puzzle from a photo · React, Vite, Tesseract.js
    what i built
    A web app that solves sudoku puzzles from a photo. It reads each cell with OCR, solves the puzzle with a backtracking algorithm in milliseconds, and keeps a history of past solves with difficulty ratings and times.
    see it
