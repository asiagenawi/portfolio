Advocacy, organizing & policy

what i've organized & advocated for

I care about how decisions get made and who gets a say in them. Growing up in Indianapolis, I saw how often young people were affected by policy without being part of the conversation, and I wanted to change that. This page covers my work in AI and education policy, my advocacy against youth gun violence, and my efforts to make sure young people in Indianapolis are heard on the issues that shape their lives.

  • 01 AI in education + Building Encode Justice Indiana to give students a voice on AI in their schools, and carrying that work into House Bill 1296.

    Encode Justice Indiana

    Founder and chapter lead · April 2023 to May 2025
    the gap
    AI was already in Indiana classrooms, but no one had documented how it was being used, and students had no organized way to weigh in on the rules being written about them.
    what i did
    I founded the Indiana chapter of Encode Justice, a youth-led grassroots initiative, and grew it to more than 30 students across 17 school districts.
    how
    We surveyed students, teachers, and administrators at more than 20 high schools on how AI was being used and regulated. Members brought those findings back to their own districts to advocate for written AI policies, and I wrote the resulting policy memo, which identified gaps in Indiana’s approach and recommended statewide legislation.
    where it landed
    The memo was published in Encode’s Spring 2024 StateAI Collection, and its recommendations became the basis for House Bill 1296. Beyond Indiana, I worked with Encode’s leadership and other states’ chapter leads on AI 2030, a youth-led global platform for safe and equitable AI governance, backed by figures including Mary Robinson, the former UN High Commissioner for Human Rights.

    Indiana House Bill 1296

    Author and advocate · March 2024 to May 2025
    the gap
    Indiana schools were adopting AI without written policies. There was no statewide guidance on which tools belonged in classrooms, students had no clear way to know what counted as acceptable use, and they could be penalized based on an AI detector’s output alone.
    what i did
    I drafted the initial bill language and pitched it to more than 15 state legislators across both chambers. Rep. Sue Errington agreed to carry it, and together we turned the draft into HB 1296. I then met with the officials who would be responsible for putting it into practice, including the House Education Committee chair and leaders from the Indiana Department of Education’s digital learning and legal teams, to build support for why it was needed.
    the bill

    HB 1296 was introduced in the Indiana House in January 2025, authored by Rep. Sue Errington and coauthored by Reps. Cherrish Pryor and Tonya Pfaff. It would have created:

    • A policy in every school. A requirement that every school corporation and charter school adopt an AI policy listing approved platforms, restrictions, and disclosure requirements, then post it publicly and share it with students.
    • State guidance for schools. Guidelines and a model AI policy covering proper and improper use with examples, each platform’s privacy policies, student disclosure requirements, and recommendations on restricting student use.
    • Protection from AI detectors. A ban on any school or school employee penalizing a student based solely on evidence from an AI detector.
    • Research across Indiana. An annual survey of teachers and students in urban, suburban, and rural schools on how AI is used, whether training works, and how AI affects teaching and learning, reported each year to the governor, the legislative council, and the state’s AI task force.
    • Resources for families. AI educational materials for students and parents, published on the Department of Education’s website.
    • A fair reporting process. A defined way for teachers to report AI misuse, and a formal review process for those reports in every high school.
    • A statewide AI inventory. An annually updated list, maintained by the Indiana Department of Education, of at least 20 AI platforms classified as supported or discouraged for school use, with a way for teachers and administrators to submit new ones.

    Read the bill at the Indiana General Assembly.

    beyond the bill
    This work led to StateScope, a tool I’m building to track AI-in-education legislation, executive orders, and agency guidance across all 50 states and DC.
  • 02 Youth gun violence prevention + Organizing Indianapolis’s first annual Peace Walk to put young people at the center of how our city approaches violence prevention.

    The first annual Indianapolis Peace Walk

    Youth chair · November 2023 to August 2024
    the gap
    Young people in Indianapolis live with the effects of gun violence, but they were rarely part of the conversation about preventing it. National Youth Violence Prevention Week is observed across the country, yet the city had no annual event marking it.
    what i did
    As youth chair, I helped build the city’s first annual Peace Walk, bringing families, advocates, and local organizations together around youth-led violence prevention.
    how
    We built the walk alongside 8 local advocacy organizations and set up 25 resource tables so families could connect directly with groups offering support, not just listen to speakers. I gave the opening remarks on centering youth voices in violence prevention and spoke on the event’s behalf to press and news outlets. We also petitioned the Mayor’s Office to formally recognize National Youth Violence Prevention Week, and I later addressed legislators at the Indiana Statehouse on MCCOY Youth Day about the lack of youth violence prevention legislation.
    where it landed
    More than 400 people came to the first walk, and the Mayor’s Office issued a proclamation recognizing National Youth Violence Prevention Week in Indianapolis. The Peace Walk has been held every year since. WRTV covered it.
  • 03 Indiana youth representation + Bringing young people’s perspectives directly to city leaders, from the Mayor’s Youth Leadership Council to the MCCOY Closing the Gap Symposium.

    Mayor’s Youth Leadership Council

    Council member · May 2023 to June 2025
    the gap
    Decisions about young people in Indianapolis are often made without young people in the room.
    what i did
    I was one of 20 students selected to represent the voice of Indianapolis youth on the Mayor’s Youth Leadership Council, which advises the mayor and city government on the issues that affect young people. Over two years, I brought the concerns of young people in my community directly to city leaders.

    MCCOY Closing the Gap Symposium

    Speaker · 2024
    the gap
    Schools and community organizations were working on the same challenges facing students, from mental health to violence, but rarely together, and rarely with students’ input.
    what i did
    I spoke at the Closing the Gap Symposium, bringing a student perspective to school leaders and community partners working to strengthen support for young people.

All of this surrounds one question: how can we build systems that meet people where they are and truly design for the diversity of the human experience?
